Frontier model benchmarks, ranked

36 benchmarks, 60+ models, checked 29 September 2026. Scores come from vendor launch tables (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, Moonshot, DeepSeek, Xiaomi, SpaceXAI), aggregated boards (llm-stats, updated Sept 28), and independent evaluators (Artificial Analysis, LMArena, Vals, Scale). Each chart is sorted best to worst. Tap a company to highlight its models.

Verdict

Computed from head-to-head results: every time two models appear on the same chart, the higher score wins that matchup. A model's rating is its share of matchups won, so it doesn't matter that charts use different scales or that no model appears on every chart. Ranking uses a confidence-adjusted version of that share, so a model tested on only a few charts can't top a list on a lucky streak. Only models with at least 6 charts are ranked overall.

Benchmarks led

How many charts below each model tops. Rough guide only — a model can only win charts it was tested on.

Same model, different numbers: Opus 5 scores 52.3, 52.6 or 51.8 on Terminal-Bench 4.0 depending on who ran it, and GPT-5.6 Sol scores 37.3 or 39.9. Artificial Analysis' independent Terminal-Bench 4.0 run (mini-swe-agent) scores Sonnet 5.5 at 63.6 and Opus 5.5 at 59.6, about 7 points under Anthropic's own numbers. AutomationBench is shown twice because Zapier's and DeepSeek's setups produce very different scales.

Terminal-Bench 2.1 and 4.0 are different tests. SWE-bench Pro mixes vendor runs with Scale's standardized runs, which read about 20 points lower; the Opus 5.5 SWE-bench Pro figure is a self-reported llm-stats entry. SWE-bench Verified is archived at Vals and only covers older models. AIME 2026, MMLU-Pro and AA-LCR mostly have open-model entries — closed labs stopped reporting them.

The head-to-head rating rewards breadth and doesn't weigh benchmark importance or price. Models that skip hard charts (for example most Chinese labs on Terminal-Bench 4.0) can look better than they are. Read it next to the per-chart detail, not instead of it.
